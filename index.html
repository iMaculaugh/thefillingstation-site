Who we are

One home base. Churches in garages, kitchens and driveways.

The Filling Station is built to grow bigger and smaller at the same time. We gather big through bike nights, group rides and outreach events. We grow small through home churches led by trained Road Crews, where riders are known, cared for, discipled and sent.

Home churches

Why we exist

To reach the one rider with the hope of Jesus, until every rider has heard.

A lot of riders are surrounded by people and still alone. Some have been hurt by churches. Some are skeptical. Some have never had a group of Christians they could trust. The motorcycle starts the conversation. Following Jesus gives it direction.

We are not trying to build an event people attend. We are building a road people can travel.

  1. First EncounterYou come for bikes, food or a ride, and you're welcomed.
  2. RelationshipYou come back and become known.
  3. Gospel MomentYou hear real stories and the hope of Jesus.
  4. Baptism & ChurchYou take a public step of faith with a family.
  5. Brotherhood & FreedomYou find healing, prayer and accountability.
  6. Road CrewYou grow in Scripture and community each week.
  7. Serve TeamYou start helping reach the next rider.
  8. Sent RiderYou lead others down the same road.

What to expect

Your first night with a crew

  1. Welcome and fellowship. Coffee, food, a little bench racing. Nobody puts you on the spot.
  2. The activity. A ride, a bike project, a meal or a service project, depending on the crew.
  3. Scripture. A short passage and a few honest questions. Church words get explained.
  4. Prayer. Share what you want. Listening is fine too.
  5. Next time. You'll leave knowing when and where the crew meets again.

Crew types

Six kinds of crews. Same road.

Every crew gathers for brotherhood, an activity, prayer and Scripture. What changes is what you do together. Pick the one that sounds like you, or try a couple. Each card takes you straight to crews of that type.

Ride Crew

Ride, then talk

A group ride with a stop for prayer, a short passage and real conversation.

Good fit if
You think best with the wind in your face and talk easiest over breakfast or at the overlook.
A typical gathering
Diner meetup, pre-ride briefing, a loop with a lead and sweep rider, Scripture and prayer at the stop.
Garage Crew

Wrench and Word

Bike maintenance, builds and projects, with a spiritual discussion worked in.

Good fit if
You'd rather have your hands busy than sit in a circle. New riders learn, experienced riders teach.
A typical gathering
Shop time on someone's bike, a break for food, a passage and a few questions, prayer before heading out.
Bible Crew

Go deeper

A more traditional Bible study for riders who want more Scripture and discipleship.

Good fit if
You're ready to dig into a book of the Bible and grow in your faith week to week.
A typical gathering
Coffee, a longer passage, open discussion with plenty of time for questions, and prayer for each other.
Outreach Crew

Reach the next rider

Serving the community, praying for people, supporting events and reaching riders.

Good fit if
You want your faith to have work boots on. You like rallies, bike nights and helping out.
A typical gathering
Prayer and a short devotional, then a service project, event support or welcome team at a bike night.
Freedom Crew

Healing and freedom

Support for riders walking through addiction, grief, anger, shame or church hurt.

Good fit if
You're carrying something heavy and need people who won't judge you and won't leave you alone with it.
A typical gathering
A safe, confidential circle, Scripture on hope and freedom, honest sharing, prayer, and help finding next steps.

Led carefully with pastoral oversight. Not a replacement for counseling or recovery programs.

Family Ride Crew

Bring everybody

Spouses, kids, cookouts, shorter rides and discipleship together as families.

Good fit if
Riding is a family thing for you, or you want your kids to see faith lived out around the bikes.
A typical gathering
A short ride, a cookout at the park, a kid-friendly Bible story and family prayer.

Lead a Road Crew

You don't need to be polished. You need to live what you lead.

Road Crew leaders host home churches built around what riders already do: rides, garage nights, meals and service. You help people connect in community, protect one another through care, and grow in Jesus.

Fill out this form to get the Road Crew Leader Field Guide so you can read it before we talk.

Every leader should be

  • A follower of Jesus, connected to a local church
  • Aligned with The Filling Station's mission
  • Faithful, dependable, safe and responsible
  • Humble, teachable and willing to pray with people
  • Able to keep confidence and to ask for help

The road to launch

  1. Apply. Tell us about you and who you hope to reach.
  2. Get the Field Guide. Read the training materials.
  3. Talk with leadership. Mission, format, location and ride safety plan.
  4. Background check when needed, then approval.
  5. Launch your crew. Keep it simple and repeatable.

Church partners

Help your church reach riders who may never walk through your doors.

Many riders are unchurched, skeptical, or carry hurt from a past church experience. They're in your community, at the gas station, the diner and the bike night down the road. The Filling Station goes where they already are.

We're a bridge to the local church, not a replacement for it. When a rider says yes to Jesus, we walk with them toward baptism and a healthy church family. Partnering with us is one way your church can be part of that.

Why partner

What partnership does for your church

Reach the one

Connect with riders and riding families your church may not reach on a regular Sunday.

Grow your church family

New believers need a home church. We point them to local churches for baptism, teaching and community.

Give your riders a mission

The riders already in your pews can serve, lead a Road Crew, or join an outreach team.

Ways to partner

How it works

Start with a conversation.

  1. Reach out. Tell us about your church and what interests you.
  2. Meet with leadership. We'll talk through vision, beliefs and what partnership could look like.
  3. Plan together. Pick dates, set expectations and decide who does what.
  4. Hear the stories. We'll report back on riders reached, lives changed and how your support was used.
